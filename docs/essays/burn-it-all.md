# Burn It All: Designing Social Protocols for a Post-Truth Internet

We’ve all been told the internet is forever. That every post, every photo, every mistake becomes part of the record—etched into some corporate archive, scraped into search engines, preserved for the worst possible context.

That permanence was never consented to. It was architected in. And it's been used to punish, shame, and erase people, not uplift them.

What if we built something else entirely? What if we started from the opposite assumption: that people grow, change, and sometimes need to disappear?

BlockParty is a protocol designed for that world. It doesn't just encrypt your data or decentralize your connections—it rethinks what identity even means. In BlockParty, your identity is a root keypair. Everything you do—every message, every post, every friendship—is signed by that identity. But if you ever decide to burn your root key, everything you ever said becomes suspect.

Not invalid. Not erased. Just… unverifiable. Maybe you wrote that post. Maybe someone else forged it. The truth dissolves. You step outside the timeline. You're free.

We call it the Post-Truth State.

It's not just a technical feature. It's a moral one.

It means you’re not chained to the worst version of yourself. It means a teenager’s stupid mistake doesn’t have to become a lifelong reputation. It means systems can be built with *forgiveness by default*, not punishment by design.

This is more than privacy. This is about **dignity**. The dignity of walking away. The dignity of silence. The dignity of *not* being watched, not being catalogued, not being reduced to metadata and ad profiles.

In a Post-Truth Internet, your past is yours to hold—or to let go. And in a world increasingly defined by surveillance, cancellation, and permanent records, that's a kind of freedom worth building for.

So yes. Burn it all. Not out of fear. But because you can.

Because protocols should reflect people—not the platforms that control them.

Because truth isn’t a blockchain—it’s a choice.

Because the future should belong to the living, not the cached.

BlockParty isn’t just a way to communicate. It’s a new social contract.

One where you always have an exit.

And one where the record can forget—when you decide it should.
