# Hobbes and BlockParty

Thomas Hobbes, best known for his work *Leviathan*, envisioned a natural state of humanity as one of chaos, conflict, and insecurity. To escape the "war of all against all," he argued, individuals would rationally surrender their rights to a sovereign power — a Leviathan — that could impose order, adjudicate disputes, and enforce contracts.

At first glance, BlockParty seems almost opposite to Hobbes' vision. It offers no Leviathan, no centralized sovereign, no forceful resolution of conflict. And yet, in a deeper sense, BlockParty answers the same problem Hobbes sought to solve — not by imposing authority, but by enabling voluntary, verifiable cooperation among free individuals.

## The State of Nature and Delay-Tolerant Order

Hobbes feared that without authority, humanity would fall into endless conflict. BlockParty acknowledges that conflict, divergence, and disorder are natural conditions — but it does not presume that centralized force is the only solution.

Instead, BlockParty builds a world where disputes, forks, disagreements, and competing truths are expected, visible, and manageable without coercion. Delay-tolerant signatures, voluntary audiences, and verifiable actions create an ecosystem where order emerges not from decree, but from consent and coordination.

## Consent Without Coercion

Hobbes saw consent as a surrender to authority; BlockParty treats consent as the continuous, revocable foundation of all association.

In BlockParty, there is no once-for-all surrender to a sovereign. Instead, individuals consent to worlds, audiences, and agreements one block at a time, one relationship at a time. Association is voluntary, dynamic, and modular, not a blanket submission to external power.

## Conflict and Forks

BlockParty assumes that forks, disputes, and competing realities will occur — just as Hobbes assumed conflict was inevitable in the natural state. But BlockParty does not view forks as failures. They are simply facts: reflections of human diversity, error, and difference.

Instead of enforcing a single authoritative chain of truth, BlockParty provides tools to navigate forks: confirmations, rejections, voluntary consensus. Order, when it exists, is an emergent phenomenon, not an imposed one.

## A Different Leviathan

In a sense, BlockParty reimagines the Leviathan. It does not create a sovereign ruler. Instead, the Leviathan becomes the voluntary web of signatures, proofs, associations, and agreements that individuals weave together.

The security Hobbes sought — protection from arbitrary violence and betrayal — is provided in BlockParty not by force, but by cryptographic integrity, verifiable consent, and the delay-tolerant durability of human intent.

## Conclusion

BlockParty does not deny Hobbes' insight that human society must solve the problem of conflict and cooperation. It simply proposes a different solution.

Instead of centralizing authority to command peace, BlockParty decentralizes authority to empower consent. Instead of fearing forks, it embraces them. Instead of building a Leviathan of flesh and blood, it builds a Leviathan of voluntary proof and action.

Where Hobbes gave us the theory of sovereignty, BlockParty offers a new canvas: a world where sovereignty remains distributed among those who act, speak, and consent — freely, and without fear.
