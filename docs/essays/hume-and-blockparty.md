# Hume and BlockParty

David Hume, the great Scottish Enlightenment thinker, approached human society with profound skepticism about abstract theories of governance. While philosophers like Hobbes and Locke spoke of social contracts as explicit, foundational events, Hume argued that most societies evolve gradually, through habit, shared practices, and the accumulation of trust — not by formal agreement.

BlockParty, though inspired by the language of consent and social contracts, aligns deeply with Hume’s pragmatic view. It does not enforce a singular, abstract contract upon its participants. Instead, it provides a minimal framework where habits of cooperation, communication, trust, and voluntary association can emerge organically, shaped by lived human action rather than by imposed design.

## Custom, Practice, and Delay-Tolerant Cooperation

Hume emphasized that much of what binds societies together is *custom* — repeated practices and expectations, developed slowly over time. BlockParty's architecture mirrors this. Worlds, audiences, and associations are not hard-coded at the protocol level. They are *practices* built by participants: norms that arise by use, not decree.

Audiences form around mutual interests. Worlds evolve consensus mechanisms suitable to their participants. Validation, trust, and meaning accrue through iterative human engagement, not from a preordained formal contract.

## Skepticism of Rationalist Constructs

Hume warned against overreliance on purely rationalist or theoretical systems detached from human reality. BlockParty shares this wariness. It does not attempt to impose a "perfect" model of governance, economy, or communication.

Instead, BlockParty accepts human messiness: delay, forked histories, conflicting truths, partial synchronizations. It provides minimal guarantees — signed intent, verifiable authorship — and leaves room for the organic emergence of higher-order structures through lived experience.

## Trust as Emergent, Not Imposed

Hume's theory of knowledge and society was deeply empirical: knowledge, trust, and authority grow through experience, not through syllogism or fiat. In BlockParty, trust is similarly emergent. There are no forced validators, no imposed authorities.

Participants trust worlds, audiences, identities, and artifacts because they have seen them behave reliably — or because social proof among peers encourages it. Trust in BlockParty is a decentralized, evolving phenomenon, not an engineered feature.

## Memory, Divergence, and Living Histories

Hume recognized that human memory is fallible, and that history itself is a constructed narrative shaped by imperfect recollection and selective recording. BlockParty embraces this reality rather than denying it.

In BlockParty, histories can fork. Memories can diverge. Participants must build and rebuild shared truths through voluntary synchronization, acknowledgment, and trust.

Instead of pretending that consensus is instant or inevitable, BlockParty provides a durable substrate where continuity, trust, and historical coherence can emerge organically over time — even across delays, disputes, and imperfect recollection.

## Consent Without Illusions

Where Hobbes and Locke imagined a moment of explicit contract, Hume argued that most people simply find themselves living under a government and accept it through habit, inertia, or prudence.

BlockParty embraces the best of this realism while restoring the possibility of conscious consent. Participation in any world, audience, or association is voluntary — and revocable. There is no assumption of permanent allegiance. Instead of assuming or mythologizing consent, BlockParty enables it to be lived, renewed, or withdrawn, delay-tolerantly and visibly.

## Conclusion

BlockParty does not seek to engineer a perfect social order. It does not impose an idealized social contract. Like Hume, it respects the organic, habitual, experimental nature of human society.

By minimizing assumptions, maximizing voluntary association, and enabling trust to emerge through lived interaction, BlockParty operationalizes a Humean vision of decentralized, pragmatic cooperation.

It is not a utopia built from theory. It is a foundation where imperfect, evolving, resilient human worlds can build themselves — one block, one signature, one consent at a time.
