# Mises and BlockParty

Ludwig von Mises, a towering figure of the Austrian School of economics, championed human action as the core unit of economic understanding. His work emphasized that all social phenomena — trade, culture, institutions — arise from the purposeful actions of individuals seeking to improve their conditions according to subjective value.

BlockParty, in its essence, can be seen as an infrastructural realization of Misesian philosophy. It is a delay-tolerant, voluntary, decentralized framework that recognizes the primacy of human action, subjective value, and free association without coercive imposition.

## Praxeology and Signed Expression

Mises built his economic theory on *praxeology*: the study of human action. Every action, he argued, implies a preference — the desire to move from a less satisfactory state to a more satisfactory one.

In BlockParty, each block is a signed act of intent: a crystallization of preference, choice, and action. Whether it is a message, a transaction, an agreement, or a collaboration, the protocol records these actions without interpretation, judgment, or coercion.

BlockParty's neutral structure parallels Mises' view that economics must not impose value judgments but simply observe that individuals act purposefully.

## Voluntary Association and Decentralized Worlds

For Mises, society was not an engineered machine but an emergent order arising from voluntary cooperation. Similarly, BlockParty's worlds, audiences, and extensions are not decreed from above — they emerge when individuals choose to associate based on perceived mutual benefit.

There is no "one size fits all" governance in BlockParty, just as there is no single correct economy in Mises' view. Each cluster, each world, each culture evolves according to the subjective values of its participants.

## Property, Exchange, and Subjective Value

Mises emphasized that all economic value is subjective. BlockParty's bearer assets and chainlet extensions operationalize this: individuals create, possess, and exchange digital artifacts based not on imposed worth but on voluntary mutual recognition.

There are no prices enforced by fiat within BlockParty. Value arises where two or more participants choose to treat an artifact, an idea, or a promise as meaningful.

## Resistance to Central Planning

Mises famously critiqued central planning as doomed to fail because planners cannot access the dispersed, subjective knowledge held by individuals. BlockParty embodies this critique at the protocol level: it refuses centralized control or enforced hierarchies.

Instead of dictating "what must happen," BlockParty creates the conditions where **anything may happen**, through voluntary, discoverable, self-organizing action.

## Conclusion

BlockParty is not an economic system; it is a substrate for all systems of action. In this, it mirrors Mises' deepest insight: that human flourishing depends on the freedom of individuals to act, associate, and value according to their own purposes.

Where Mises provided the theoretical foundation for understanding free societies, BlockParty offers a technological foundation for building them — one voluntary act at a time.
